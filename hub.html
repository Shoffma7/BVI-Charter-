Boat rules

The captain will go over these at the briefing. Reading them first saves the first day.

Heads
Marine toilets clog easily. Follow the charter company's rule at the briefing. The safe default is nothing in the toilet except what came out of you, and paper goes in the bin.
Water
Short showers even with the watermaker. Nothing greasy down the galley sink.
Shoes
Non-slip soles, no dark soles (they mark the deck). Flip-flops with no heel strap go overboard.
Dinghy
Tie it with two lines. Know where the kill switch is. Make two trips rather than overloading it. Pull it up on the lift at night.
Trash
Nothing goes overboard. Bag it daily and drop it at marinas.
Sun
Reapply every two hours. Overcast days burn too. Day one is when somebody gets cooked.
Quiet hours
Sound carries on a catamaran. Agree on a time at the first dinner.

Moorings

How they work
First come, first served, roughly $30–40 a night, paid to the mooring host who comes by dinghy. Popular spots fill by early afternoon.
Our routine
Leave the anchorage by about 9am. Pick tomorrow's stop the night before.
The Baths
Be there before 9am if we can.
Anegada
Call ahead on VHF to the mooring host before arriving, and stay in the marked channel.
Cash
Bring small US bills. Mooring hosts and some beach bars take cash only.
